THE BOTTLENECK

Web content shouldn't require a project every time.

For most established businesses, a simple site change means a developer ticket, an agency invoice, or a page that just sits stale because updating it is too much friction. The content that represents the company ends up being the hardest thing to change.

  • Small edits wait days behind a dev queue
  • Agencies bill by the hour for one-line changes
  • No clear history of what changed, when, or why
  • Content drifts out of date because updates are painful
  • You can't move your site without re-hiring the vendor
  • "Who has the latest version?" is a real question
HOW IT WORKS

From a plain-English request to a live page — in one short loop.

AI does the drafting and editing. Git and GitHub provide the safety net. Nothing goes live without a human review.

Step 01

Describe it

You ask in plain English — "add a services page," "refresh the homepage headline," "publish this announcement."

Step 02

AI drafts & edits

Claude Code writes and edits the actual page files on a Windows machine — matching your existing style, structure, and brand.

Step 03

Commit & review

Each change is committed to GitHub — a timestamped, reversible record you can read, approve, or roll back at any point.

Step 04

Auto-deploy

On approval, GitHub Pages (or your host) publishes the change automatically — usually live within a minute or two.
